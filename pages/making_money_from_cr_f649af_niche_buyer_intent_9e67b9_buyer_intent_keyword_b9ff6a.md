## Introduction

Affiliate websites earn revenue when they attract visitors who are already moving towards a purchase, not simply gathering information. The most valuable keywords therefore reveal buying behaviour rather than general curiosity. Search phrases containing commercial modifiers such as *best*, *review*, *vs*, *alternative*, *price*, or a specific product model often indicate that the searcher is evaluating options and is much closer to making a decision than someone searching for a basic explanation. ## Commercial Modifiers That Matter

Individual words rarely guarantee purchase intent, but certain modifiers consistently appear near buying decisions.

**"Best"**

Searchers have already decided to buy a type of product and are choosing which one. This is the classic affiliate keyword because readers expect independent comparisons.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* best hiking backpack
* best cordless vacuum for pet hair
* best accounting software for freelancers

</div>

Adding a use case ("for pet hair", "for freelancers") generally creates more specific intent and reduces competition.

**"Review"**

Someone searching for a review usually wants confirmation before purchasing.

Examples include:

* Oura Ring review
* NordVPN review
* standing desk review

These searches reward detailed first-hand [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) rather than rewritten specifications.

**"Vs" and "Compare"**

Comparison searches reveal that the buying shortlist has already been narrowed.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Kindle Paperwhite vs Kobo Clara
* ClickUp vs Asana
* Garmin Fenix vs Epix

</div>

These visitors often need only a small amount of evidence before making a decision.

**"Alternative"**

Alternative keywords appear when users are dissatisfied with a current product, concerned about pricing, or evaluating competitors.

Examples:

* Notion alternatives
* Mailchimp alternatives
* Grammarly alternative

These pages naturally fit affiliate recommendations because readers are actively looking to switch.

**Price-related modifiers**

Keywords including:

* price
* cost
* discount
* coupon
* deal

usually indicate movement from evaluation towards purchase. However, they also attract strong competition from retailers and voucher sites.

**Problem-solving modifiers**

Questions such as:

* is X worth it
* is X reliable
* X problems
* X battery life
* X [durability]({{ 'durability/' | relative_url }})

often appear immediately before purchase because buyers are trying to eliminate uncertainty rather than learn basic facts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/buyer-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ahrefs.com">[Ahrefs+2Semrush]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ahrefs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Buyer Intent Keywords Convert Better</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here&#x27;s How to Find...July 30, 2024 — 30 Jul 2024 — The most reliable way to find buyer-intent keywords is to use an SEO tool that allows...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2024</span></span></span>

Rather than relying on instinct, classify potential keywords into clear groups before deciding whether a niche has affiliate potential.

## Where Weak Niches Hide Free-Information Traffic

Large search volumes can create the illusion of a profitable niche while producing little affiliate income.

Consider the difference between these searches:

SearchLikely intenthow coffee is grownLearninghistory of coffeeLearningcoffee bean typesResearchbest espresso grinder under £300BuyingBaratza Encore ESP reviewBuyingEureka Mignon vs Niche ZeroBuying

The first group may generate substantial [traffic]({{ 'traffic/' | relative_url }}) but offers relatively few natural opportunities for affiliate recommendations. The second group represents visitors actively narrowing purchasing choices.

Weak affiliate niches often contain an overwhelming proportion of searches beginning with:

* what is
* definition
* history
* why
* when
* free
* examples
* template

These topics can build readership but frequently attract users with no immediate purchasing objective.

Strong niches instead contain hundreds of variations around:

* best
* review
* compare
* alternative
* for...
* under £...
* worth it
* model names
* feature comparisons

The difference is not merely semantic; it reflects where readers sit within the buying journey.

## Long-Tail Keywords Often Reveal the Strongest Purchase Signals

Long-tail keywords—more detailed searches of three or more words—frequently combine [buyer intent]({{ 'buyer-intent/' | relative_url }}) with lower competition.

Instead of targeting:

* best laptop

a stronger affiliate opportunity might be:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* best lightweight laptop for engineering students
* best laptop under £1,000 for video editing
* MacBook Air M3 vs Dell XPS 13

</div>

These searches reveal:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* a defined budget
* a specific use case
* narrowed product categories
* immediate decision-making

</div>

## Looking Beyond Keyword Modifiers

Commercial words are useful indicators, but they are not the only evidence of buyer intent.

Experienced affiliate researchers also examine search engine results pages (SERPs). Google often reinforces commercial intent by displaying:

* Shopping advertisements
* Product carousels
* Review [snippets]({{ 'snippets/' | relative_url }})
* Popular products panels
* Comparison articles
* Retail category pages

The presence of these features suggests that both advertisers and Google's ranking systems recognise commercial value in the query, even if traditional modifiers such as "best" or "review" are absent.
