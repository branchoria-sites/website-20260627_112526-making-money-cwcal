## Introduction

A trustworthy [software]({{ 'software/' | relative_url }}) review should answer one uncomfortable question before it recommends any subscription service: **can a business leave with its own data?** For small companies, changing accounting software, a CRM, an ecommerce platform or a booking system is difficult enough without discovering that customer records, invoices or sales history cannot be exported in a practical format.

For affiliate publishers, this is more than a technical detail. Recommending software that traps customer data can damage reader trust long after an [affiliate commission]({{ 'disclosure-967c81/' | relative_url }}) has been earned. The best reviews therefore test data exports alongside features, pricing and [integrations]({{ 'integrations/' | relative_url }}). Rather than asking simply whether an export button exists, they examine whether the exported information is complete, reusable and realistic for migration to another platform. Current privacy law and emerging European rules on cloud switching have also increased expectations that businesses should be able to retrieve their information in structured, machine-readable formats rather than being locked into proprietary systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kukie.io/blog/right-to-data-portability-saas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kukie.io">[Kukie.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kukie.io</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn what data you must export, in which formats, and by when</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Portability for SaaS: GDPR &amp; EU Data Act Guide &#124; Kukie.ioMarch 18, 2026 — GDPR Article 20 and the EU Data Act create data portabilit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 18, 2026</span></span></span>

## Which records should every review test?

Many software reviews mention "CSV export" without checking what is actually included. In practice, different SaaS products export very different amounts of information.

A useful review should verify whether the following business records can be exported:

* Customer and contact records, including custom fields.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Products, services and stock information.
* Orders, quotations and invoices.
* Payment history where applicable.
* Customer communications or notes.
* Reporting data and transaction history.
* User accounts, permissions and audit logs where relevant.
* Attachments or uploaded documents.
* Tags, categories and other organisational metadata.

</div>

[Testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) should also establish whether relationships survive the export. For example, exporting customers and invoices separately is far less useful if invoice records no longer identify the correct customer. Likewise, an ecommerce export should preserve product variants, stock keeping units (SKUs), categories and order relationships rather than reducing everything to disconnected spreadsheets.

For affiliate content, screenshots of successful exports and sample column headings provide stronger evidence than simply repeating a vendor's marketing claims.

## Usable exports versus locked reports

Not every download is genuinely portable.

Some SaaS products allow users to download attractive PDF reports while preventing access to the underlying transactional data. A monthly sales PDF may satisfy reporting needs, but it is almost useless for migrating to another accounting or ecommerce platform.

When reviewing software, distinguish between:

Useful exportLimited exportCSV, JSON or XML filesPDF reports onlyComplete datasetsSummary reportsPreserves IDs and relationshipsFlattened or aggregated informationCan be imported elsewhereDesigned only for readingIncludes historical recordsIncludes only recent activity

European data portability guidance consistently refers to structured, commonly used and machine-readable formats. CSV and JSON generally meet this expectation, while PDF alone normally does not because it cannot easily be imported into another application.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kukie.io/blog/right-to-data-portability-saas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kukie.io">[Kukie.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kukie.io</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn what data you must export, in which formats, and by when</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Portability for SaaS: GDPR &amp; EU Data Act Guide &#124; Kukie.ioMarch 18, 2026 — GDPR Article 20 and the EU Data Act create data portabilit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Another practical check is whether column names are understandable. Exports filled with internal database identifiers or undocumented abbreviations may technically satisfy an export function while remaining difficult for customers to reuse.

## How export limits affect affiliate trust

Affiliate publishers benefit when readers believe recommendations remain honest after the commission has been paid.

Ignoring export limitations creates several problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Readers may discover migration barriers months after signing up.
* Businesses can face unexpected costs for manual data reconstruction.
* Switching providers becomes more expensive than expected.
* Readers lose confidence in future recommendations from the same website.

</div>

By contrast, reviews that openly discuss export strengths and weaknesses demonstrate independence. Saying that a CRM exports contacts perfectly but omits automation history is often more persuasive than claiming every feature is excellent.

This approach also reduces the appearance that recommendations exist solely to maximise [affiliate revenue]({{ 'revenue-math/' | relative_url }}).

## Questions worth testing before recommending any SaaS product

Instead of relying on specification sheets, perform practical checks wherever possible.

Useful review questions include:

* Can all customer records be exported?
* Are invoices and financial history included?

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Are products, inventory and categories preserved?
* Does the export include custom fields?
* Are historical transactions available or only current records?
* Are attachments downloadable?
* Can exports be generated on lower-priced plans?
* Is there an API for large-scale exports?
* Does the system document the export format?
* Is the exported data easy to import into competing software?

</div>

These tests often reveal differences that pricing [tables]({{ 'tables/' | relative_url }}) never mention.

## Watch for hidden restrictions

Export capability is sometimes limited in ways that are easy to overlook during a trial.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Export functions available only on premium subscriptions.
* Limits on the number of records exported.
* Rate limits that slow large downloads.
* Missing archived or deleted records.
* Custom fields excluded from exports.
* Manual support requests required for complete account exports.
* Extra charges for migration assistance.

</div>

These [restrictions]({{ 'restrictions/' | relative_url }}) deserve explicit mention because they affect the real cost of switching providers.

## Data portability is becoming a competitive feature

Regulation increasingly encourages software providers to reduce customer lock-in rather than depend on it.

Under the GDPR, qualifying individuals have rights to receive certain personal data in structured, commonly used and machine-readable formats under defined conditions. Alongside this, the EU Data Act introduces broader switching obligations for many cloud and SaaS providers, aiming to reduce barriers that prevent organisations moving between services. These developments reinforce the commercial value of transparent export tools rather than treating them as optional extras.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kukie.io/blog/right-to-data-portability-saas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kukie.io">[Kukie.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kukie.io</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn what data you must export, in which formats, and by when</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Portability for SaaS: GDPR &amp; EU Data Act Guide &#124; Kukie.ioMarch 18, 2026 — GDPR Article 20 and the EU Data Act create data portabilit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Even outside legal requirements, enterprise procurement teams frequently assess data portability before adopting new software because they want confidence that future migrations will remain practical. Transparent export capabilities therefore become a trust signal rather than merely a compliance feature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://veldsystems.com/blog/saas-data-export-portability" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: veldsystems.com">[veldsystems.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">veldsystems.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saa S Data Export and Portability: What to Build and Why It</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Learn how to build robust data portability features that reduce churn, build trust, and meet...</span></span></span>

## What to include in an affiliate review

For readers comparing business software, a concise "Data Export" section adds far more value than another feature list.

A high-quality review should state:

* Which business records were successfully exported.
* Which formats were available.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://kukie.io/blog/right-to-data-portability-saas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kukie.io">[kukie.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kukie.io</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn what data you must export, in which formats, and by when</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Portability for SaaS: GDPR &amp; EU Data Act Guide &#124; Kukie.ioMarch 18, 2026 — GDPR Article 20 and the EU Data Act create data portabilit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 18, 2026</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Whether exports preserved relationships between records.
* Any plan-based restrictions.
* Whether exports were tested personally or documented by the vendor.
* Any important omissions that could complicate migration.

</div>
