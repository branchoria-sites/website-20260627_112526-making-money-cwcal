## Introduction

Fixed bounties are affiliate payments that reward a specific action with a predetermined amount rather than a percentage of a sale. Instead of earning more when a customer spends more, you receive the same commission whenever a visitor completes a qualifying event such as starting a free trial, requesting a quote, opening an account, installing an app, or submitting an approved lead. For many affiliate websites, this can produce more predictable earnings and, in the right niche, outperform percentage-based [commissions]({{ 'commissions/' | relative_url }}) because the required action is easier for visitors to complete. Cost-per-action (CPA) and cost-per-lead (CPL) programmes have become especially common in [software]({{ 'software/' | relative_url }}), finance, insurance and business services, where merchants place a high value on acquiring new prospects. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.businessofapps.com/affiliate/cpl/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: businessofapps.com">[Business of Apps]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">businessofapps.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Business of AppsTop CPL Affiliate Networks (2026)3 days ago — CPL (Cost-Per-Lead) is a model in which the advertiser pays a fixed price f...</span></span></span>

## Can a Small Bounty Beat a Big Sale?

The headline payout alone rarely tells the full story. A £100 commission sounds attractive, but if only one visitor in 500 converts, it may earn less overall than a £10 bounty that converts one visitor in 20.

Consider two simplified examples:

* A product review pays 10% on a £500 purchase, earning £50 per sale. If 1 in 200 visitors buys, the expected return is around £0.25 per visitor.
* A software comparison page pays £8 whenever someone starts a verified free trial. If 1 in 15 visitors signs up, the expected return is roughly £0.53 per visitor.

Although the second commission is much smaller, the easier conversion can produce higher revenue from the same amount of [traffic]({{ 'traffic/' | relative_url }}). Experienced affiliates therefore evaluate expected earnings rather than headline commission percentages.

This is why many successful affiliate sites are built around pages with a single, well-defined call to action, such as "Start your free trial", "Get a quote", or "Book a demo", instead of relying only on expensive product purchases.

## What Fixed Bounties Pay For

Flat-fee affiliate programmes usually define one measurable action that demonstrates genuine customer interest. Common qualifying events include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Verified free-trial registrations.
* Qualified insurance or financial quotes.
* Approved loan or credit enquiries.
* Business software demonstrations.
* Newsletter or account registrations that meet quality rules.
* Mobile app installations with activation requirements.

</div>

The exact qualification [criteria]({{ 'criteria/' | relative_url }}) matter. A merchant may require [email]({{ 'email/' | relative_url }}) verification, completion of a profile, identity checks, or minimum usage before approving a payout. ## Why Easier Actions Can Change Expected Earnings

Flat bounties work because they reduce friction in the conversion journey.

Buying a £1,000 product requires substantial trust, budget and commitment. Starting a free software trial or requesting an insurance quotation demands far less from the visitor.

For affiliate publishers, this changes both content strategy and optimisation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Comparison pages can focus on helping readers choose between providers before asking them to begin a trial.
* Tutorials naturally end with an invitation to test the featured product.
* Problem-solving articles can recommend requesting a quote or consultation as the logical next step.

</div>

The merchant also benefits. Even if the visitor does not become an immediate paying customer, the business gains a qualified lead that can be nurtured through email marketing, sales calls or automated onboarding.

## When Flat Fees Limit Long-Term Earnings

The trade-off is that fixed bounties usually disconnect your earnings from the customer's lifetime value.

Suppose two visitors each generate a £75 bounty:

* One becomes a customer for a single month.
* The other remains a customer for five years and spends thousands of pounds.

The affiliate still earns the same £75 unless the programme includes recurring commissions, milestone bonuses or additional performance incentives.

This can make flat-fee programmes less attractive when promoting products with exceptionally high customer lifetime values, particularly premium software [subscriptions]({{ 'subscriptions/' | relative_url }}) or enterprise services. ## Choosing When to Promote Flat-Bounty Offers

Fixed bounties are often strongest when several conditions are true:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Your content attracts visitors who are actively researching a solution rather than casually browsing.
* The required action is simple enough to achieve high conversion rates.
* The merchant has a reliable approval process and transparent qualification rules.
* Your audience values guidance through a decision rather than simply finding the cheapest product.

</div>

Before joining any programme, review more than just the payout amount. Check exactly what counts as a qualified lead, whether duplicate or incomplete submissions are rejected, how long attribution cookies last, and how reversals are handled. For many affiliate websites, particularly those centred on software [reviews]({{ 'reviews/' | relative_url }}), business services, finance or insurance comparisons, a modest but consistently earned fixed bounty can generate steadier and sometimes greater overall income than chasing high-percentage commissions on infrequent sales. The key metric is not the size of each commission but the combination of payout value, conversion rate and approval quality.
