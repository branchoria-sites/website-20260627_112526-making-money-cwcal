## Introduction

Air fryer capacity is one of the most misunderstood specifications in [kitchen appliance]({{ 'appliances/' | relative_url }}) marketing. A model advertised as holding 6 or 8 litres may not cook enough chips, chicken or vegetables for four people in a single batch if the basket has to be filled in a deep pile. Because air fryers rely on fast-moving hot air, usable cooking space matters far more than the raw volume printed on the box. For affiliate [reviews]({{ 'reviews/' | relative_url }}), this is one of the clearest opportunities to add genuine value: showing what a basket can cook evenly in real use rather than repeating manufacturer specifications.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_kitchen_appliance_re_419365_air_fryer_capacity_f_363e8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Air Fryer Fit illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_kitchen_appliance_re_419365_air_fryer_capacity_f_363e8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/making_money_from_cr_f649af_kitchen_appliance_re_419365_air_fryer_capacity_f_363e8a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Readers looking to buy an air fryer are rarely asking how much empty space sits inside the drawer. They want to know whether frozen chips stay crisp, whether chicken browns evenly without constant shaking, and whether a family meal fits without cooking in two rounds. Those practical questions often reveal a large gap between advertised capacity and everyday performance.

## Advertised litres versus usable cooking space

Manufacturers usually describe basket air fryers by internal volume in litres or quarts. While this provides a rough indication of size, it says little about how much food can actually be cooked well.

The reason is simple. Air fryers cook by circulating hot air around exposed food surfaces. Once food is stacked too deeply, airflow is blocked and the appliance behaves more like a small oven than a high-speed convection cooker. Items on top brown while those underneath steam.

Independent [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) has repeatedly highlighted this distinction. Consumer organisation Which? measures practical food capacity rather than relying on the manufacturer's litre rating and reports that many air fryers offer only around 60% to 80% of their advertised capacity as genuinely usable cooking space. For affiliate reviews, this means readers benefit far more from measurements such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Weight of frozen chips cooked evenly in one layer.
* Number of chicken thighs or drumsticks that fit without overlap.
* Number of burgers or sausages that brown evenly.
* Whether a whole chicken fits comfortably.
* Whether food requires shaking every few minutes simply to compensate for overcrowding.

</div>

These tests answer the practical buying question that litre ratings alone cannot.

## Why crowded baskets produce disappointing food

Crowding affects cooking through airflow rather than heating power.

Every air fryer uses a fan to move hot air rapidly around the food. When ingredients are packed tightly together:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exposed surfaces receive less moving air;
* moisture becomes trapped between pieces;
* steam cannot escape efficiently;
* browning slows dramatically;
* cooking becomes uneven.

</div>

The result is familiar to many owners:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* chips remain pale underneath;
* breaded chicken loses crispness;
* vegetables soften instead of roasting;
* food may require repeated shaking or turning to finish evenly.

</div>

Many cooking experts and appliance test laboratories therefore recommend arranging food in a mostly single layer wherever possible. Overfilling is consistently identified as one of the biggest reasons users report disappointing results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.techradar.com/home/air-fryers/are-you-making-these-6-common-mistakes-when-using-your-air-fryer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: techradar.com">[TechRadar]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">techradar.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tech Radar Are you making these 6 common mistakes when using your air fryer?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First, relying strictly on included recipe books can lead to poor outcomes; users should experiment with temperatures and times, especial...</span></span></span>

This mechanism also explains why a larger basket is not always better if its base area is unchanged. A deeper drawer increases volume, but not necessarily the cooking surface where airflow matters most.

## Crowded basket tests with everyday foods

A useful affiliate review should test foods buyers actually cook rather than relying on laboratory-style demonstrations.

### Frozen chips

Frozen chips quickly reveal usable capacity.

A lightly filled basket generally produces crisp exteriors and fluffy centres with one or two shakes during cooking. Once the basket is overloaded, chips begin trapping steam against one another. The lower layers often remain soft even after longer cooking times.

Rather than reporting that a fryer is "6 litres", a review is more useful if it explains:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the maximum weight that stayed crisp;
* how often shaking was required;
* whether extending cooking time actually improved the result or simply dried the top layer.

</div>

### Chicken portions

Chicken is an especially revealing test because both browning and safe cooking matter.

Bone-in thighs, drumsticks and wings require hot air to reach most surfaces. When pieces touch extensively, the contact points stay pale while exposed areas brown first.

Leatherhead Food Research, which has carried out thousands of validated air fryer cooking trials for food manufacturers, notes that chicken products are among the foods consumers cook most often in air fryers and that performance varies between models because of differences in airflow and power.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sagentia.com/news/consumer-research-findings-underline-the-importance-of-air-fryer-cooking-instructions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sagentia.com">[Sagentia Consulting]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sagentia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Consulting -65% UK households own air fryer &#124; Leatherhead Food</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sagentia Consulting -65% UK households own air fryer &#124; Leatherhead Food...March 31, 2026 — Leatherhead Food Research&#x27;s latest survey on...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 31, 2026</span></span></span>

For reviewers, practical observations are valuable:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Did all pieces reach the same level of browning?
* Were thicker pieces fully cooked without burning thinner ones?
* Did the user need to rearrange the basket halfway through?

</div>

### Mixed family meals

Real households often cook combinations rather than single ingredients.

Testing sausages beside vegetables, or chicken alongside potatoes, demonstrates whether the appliance has enough exposed cooking area to avoid constant reshuffling.

When reviewers document the compromises required—such as removing one food early while another continues cooking—they provide [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) that readers cannot obtain from a specification sheet.

## Does that air fryer really feed four?

Manufacturers frequently describe medium and large air fryers as suitable for families of four. That statement can be technically true, but only for certain meals.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Four chicken breasts may fit comfortably.
* Four baked potatoes may fit comfortably.
* Four portions of chips often require careful spreading.
* A mixed meal containing protein and vegetables may exceed the practical cooking area.

</div>

This explains why owners sometimes report needing two batches despite buying what appeared to be a generously sized appliance.

A stronger review therefore avoids repeating marketing language and instead states precisely what was cooked. "Fed four" should mean an actual meal completed in one cooking cycle, not simply that the drawer could physically close.

## Choosing the right air fryer style

Capacity should be evaluated alongside basket shape rather than volume alone.

**Single-drawer basket models**

These suit one or two people particularly well because they maximise airflow across a relatively small cooking area. For couples or individuals, a well-designed medium basket often cooks more evenly than an oversized drawer filled beyond its ideal level.

**Dual-zone air fryers**

Dual-drawer designs solve a different problem. Instead of increasing one cooking chamber, they provide two independent baskets.

Advantages include:

* cooking different foods simultaneously;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.goodhousekeeping.com/appliances/a71593976/best-air-fryers-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: goodhousekeeping.com">[goodhousekeeping.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">goodhousekeeping.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Good Housekeeping After Testing 70+ Models, These Are the Best Air Fryers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">With fast heating and powerful airflow, air fryers are excellent for cooking a wide variety of foods such as proteins, vegetables, pre-pa...</span></span></span>
* separate temperatures and timings;
* avoiding flavour transfer;
* reducing overcrowding by splitting portions.

They are especially useful for households preparing protein and side dishes together rather than one very large quantity of a single food.

**Air fryer toaster ovens**

These prioritise cooking surface over basket depth.

The wider trays make them particularly suitable for foods that benefit from remaining in a single layer, such as pizza slices, fish fillets, open sandwiches and roasted vegetables. ## What affiliate reviews should measure instead of repeating litre claims

One of the easiest ways for an affiliate website to stand out is to replace marketing specifications with repeatable real-world tests.

Useful evidence includes:

* weighed portions of frozen chips cooked in one batch;
* number of chicken thighs fitting without overlap;
* browning consistency across the basket;
* number of basket shakes required;
* whether extending cooking time fixed overcrowding problems;
* photographs before and after cooking;
* comparison between [manufacturer claims]({{ 'claims-test/' | relative_url }}) and practical meal sizes.

Readers rarely regret knowing that an advertised 7-litre appliance comfortably cooks only 600–700 grams of chips if crispness is the priority. They are much more likely to regret discovering that limitation after purchase.

Capacity claims therefore become meaningful only when tied to realistic cooking performance. The most trustworthy air fryer reviews are those that demonstrate not how much food can be squeezed into the drawer, but how much can be cooked evenly, safely and well in a single batch.
