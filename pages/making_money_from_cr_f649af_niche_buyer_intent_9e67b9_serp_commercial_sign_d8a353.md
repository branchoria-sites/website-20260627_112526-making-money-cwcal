## Introduction

Search results pages (SERPs) are one of the quickest ways to judge whether an affiliate niche has genuine commercial value. Before analysing keyword volumes or commission rates, examine what Google already displays for the searches you hope to target. A results page filled with shopping advertisements, retailer listings, product carousels, comparison articles, review sites and price-focused content is strong [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) that advertisers are spending money because people searching those terms are close to making a purchase. By contrast, a page dominated by encyclopaedias, forums, definitions and educational articles often signals that users want information rather than products.

It is a live snapshot of Google's understanding of user intent and the level of commercial activity surrounding a keyword. ## Can Search Results Prove a Niche Pays?

Search engines invest heavily in matching result types to search intent. When Google believes users are researching products or comparing [alternatives]({{ 'alternatives/' | relative_url }}), it tends to introduce commercial features alongside traditional organic listings. These features exist because advertisers are willing to compete for those searches and because users frequently engage with buying-related content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.semrush.com/blog/commercial-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: semrush.com">[Semrush]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">semrush.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">commercial intent keywords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commercial Intent Keywords: The Complete Guide for...3 Dec 2025 — Commercial intent keywords are search terms used to research a...</span></span></span>

A single search result should never decide whether a niche is worth entering. Instead, examine a sample of 30 to 50 commercially relevant keywords. If similar commercial signals appear repeatedly, the niche is much more likely to support [affiliate content]({{ 'content-mix/' | relative_url }}) than one where buying-related features appear only occasionally.

## Shopping Ads and Product Carousels

The strongest visible indicator of commercial intent is the presence of shopping-oriented SERP features.

When searches consistently display shopping advertisements or product listings with images, prices, retailers and ratings, Google has determined that many users are actively evaluating products rather than simply learning about a topic. Businesses only continue bidding on these placements when the [traffic]({{ 'traffic/' | relative_url }}) converts into sales at acceptable costs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.semrush.com/blog/commercial-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: semrush.com">[Semrush]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">semrush.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">commercial intent keywords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commercial Intent Keywords: The Complete Guide for...3 Dec 2025 — Commercial intent keywords are search terms used to research a...</span></span></span>

Useful commercial signals include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Shopping advertisements at the top of the page.
* Product carousels containing multiple retailers.
* Merchant ratings and review stars.
* Product availability or price information.
* "Popular products" or similar shopping modules.

</div>

For example, a search such as "best cordless drill for home use" may display paid shopping listings before any editorial content. That suggests manufacturers and retailers expect purchasing decisions to follow the search. An informational search such as "how does a drill motor work" is far less likely to show those commercial elements.

It is also worth noting that Google's shopping experience continues to evolve, with product modules appearing in more commercial searches than in previous years. ## Review Snippets and Comparison Pages

Affiliate publishers should pay close attention to the organic results, not just the advertisements.

Commercial investigation searches often produce pages such as:

* "Best" product round-ups.
* Individual product [reviews]({{ 'reviews/' | relative_url }}).

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* "Product A vs Product B" comparisons.
* Buyer guides.
* Retail category pages.
* Professional testing organisations.
* Independent review publications.

</div>

This mix indicates that searchers are gathering evidence before committing to a purchase. Google frequently ranks third-party evaluations highly because users researching expensive or important purchases tend to prefer independent assessments over manufacturer claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.semrush.com/blog/commercial-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: semrush.com">[Semrush]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">semrush.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">commercial intent keywords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Commercial Intent Keywords: The Complete Guide for...3 Dec 2025 — Commercial intent keywords are search terms used to research a...</span></span></span>

For affiliate marketers, this is encouraging because it demonstrates that Google already considers editorial recommendation pages appropriate answers for those searches. It does not guarantee easy rankings, but it confirms that useful review content fits the search intent.

A healthy commercial SERP often contains several different perspectives:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Retailers selling products.
* Independent reviewers.
* Comparison websites.
* Specialist publications.
* Manufacturer pages.
* User discussions.

</div>

That diversity creates opportunities for high-quality affiliate content which adds testing, comparison or buying advice instead of merely repeating specifications.

The balance between result types often matters more than any individual feature.

A commercially attractive SERP usually combines several elements:

By contrast, a page dominated by dictionaries, government resources, educational articles or general definitions often reflects informational intent. Such topics may generate traffic but frequently convert poorly into [affiliate commissions]({{ 'disclosure-967c81/' | relative_url }}).

## When Search Results Show Traffic Without Buyers

Large search volumes can be misleading.

Some subjects attract millions of searches while displaying almost no commercial signals. These searches may generate advertising revenue through display ads, but they often struggle to produce affiliate income because visitors are not actively shopping.

Warning signs include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Almost no paid advertisements.
* No shopping modules.
* Organic results dominated by informational articles.
* Few product-specific searches.
* Little evidence of comparison or review content.
* Search suggestions centred on definitions rather than purchasing.

</div>

For example, a hobby or educational topic may produce extensive informational traffic without any meaningful purchasing behaviour. In contrast, a narrower search involving product comparisons, replacement parts or specialist equipment may receive far less traffic while producing significantly stronger affiliate opportunities.

The goal is not simply to find popular searches but to identify searches where buying behaviour is clearly visible in the SERP itself.

## Practical SERP Evaluation for Affiliate Niches

A repeatable review process helps separate promising niches from attractive-looking but weak opportunities.

For each candidate keyword, ask:


<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/r3nAlpXHP58" title="Keyword Intent Explained: The 4 Search Types Every Small Business Must Know" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3nAlpXHP58" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Keyword Intent Explained: The 4 Search Types Every Small Business Must Know</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: SF Digital Studios &middot; Views: 50 &middot; Uploaded: January 2026 &middot; Length: 3 minutes 17 seconds</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3nAlpXHP58" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r3nAlpXHP58">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Are paid advertisements consistently present?
2. Does Google show shopping or product listing features?
3. Are independent reviews ranking prominently?
4. Do comparison articles appear naturally?
5. Are recognised retailers competing for visibility?
6. Does the page focus on products rather than explanations?
7. Would a genuinely better buying guide fit naturally among the existing results?

</div>

If most answers are "yes" across dozens of related searches, the niche demonstrates sustained commercial intent rather than isolated buying activity.

Conversely, if nearly every keyword produces educational resources, forums and definitions with little commercial activity, the niche may depend more on informational traffic than affiliate conversions.

## Reading SERPs as Market Evidence

A search results page is effectively a public market signal. Every advertisement represents a business willing to pay for visibility. Every retailer ranking organically has invested in attracting customers. Every comparison article that continues to rank indicates that Google believes users want help choosing between products before purchasing.

For affiliate publishers, these visible patterns are valuable because they can be assessed without expensive tools. They provide evidence that money is already flowing through a market, helping distinguish niches with genuine [buyer intent]({{ 'buyer-intent/' | relative_url }}) from those that simply attract curious readers.
