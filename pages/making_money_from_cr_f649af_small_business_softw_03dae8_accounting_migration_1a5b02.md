## Introduction

Switching accounting [software]({{ 'software/' | relative_url }}) can be a sensible move for a growing small business, but it is rarely as simple as exporting one file and importing it into another. Financial records, bank feeds, unpaid invoices, tax settings and accountant access all need to continue working without interrupting day-to-day operations. A poorly planned migration can leave businesses with missing transactions, incorrect opening balances, duplicated records or reporting problems that only become obvious at tax time. Vendor guidance and accounting specialists consistently recommend treating migration as an accounting project rather than an IT task, with careful planning, [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) and reconciliation before relying on the new system. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://quickbooks.intuit.com/global/accountants-software/migrating-accounting-practice-to-accounting-software/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quickbooks.intuit.com">[QuickBooks+2numeric.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quickbooks.intuit.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here are the steps to transition your accounting practice to new accounting software: Set a data...Read more...</span></span></span>

The most valuable accounting data is often the information businesses assume will transfer automatically. In reality, different accounting platforms store information differently, and migration tools may only import selected records.

Before migration, businesses should produce a complete set of reports from the existing system, including a trial balance, balance sheet, profit and loss statement and aged receivables and payables. These become the benchmark for confirming that the new system matches the old one after conversion. Professional migration [checklists]({{ 'checklists/' | relative_url }}) consistently recommend reconciling these reports before switching off the previous system. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://quickbooks.intuit.com/global/accountants-software/migrating-accounting-practice-to-accounting-software/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quickbooks.intuit.com">[QuickBooks+2AccountingWEB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quickbooks.intuit.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here are the steps to transition your accounting practice to new accounting software: Set a data...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Bank feeds, invoices and tax records require extra attention

Many business owners assume that if transactions appear in one accounting system they will automatically continue appearing in another. Bank feeds are one of the biggest exceptions.

### Bank feeds usually need reconnecting

Direct bank connections normally rely on separate authorisations between the accounting platform and the bank. These connections generally do not transfer during migration and must be established again in the new software after the move. This means businesses should plan for a short period of verification before relying entirely on automated transaction imports. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wowbookswitch.com/blog/switching-from-quickbooks-to-xero-what-free-tools-get-wrong-on-complex-files" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wowbookswitch.com">[wowbookswitch.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wowbookswitch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wowbookswitch.com.</span></span></span>

Businesses should also confirm:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* every active bank account has been connected;
* historical imported transactions have not been duplicated;
* reconciliation rules behave as expected; and
* recurring bank matches still work correctly.

</div>

### Open invoices and outstanding payments

Outstanding customer invoices and supplier bills deserve special checking because they affect cash flow immediately after migration.

The migration should preserve:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* unpaid invoices;
* unpaid supplier bills;
* payment allocations;
* credit notes;
* outstanding customer balances.

</div>

If allocations between invoices and payments are lost, businesses can end up chasing customers who have already paid or incorrectly paying suppliers twice. ## Why testing matters more than importing

Importing data is only the middle of the migration process. The real objective is proving that financial reports remain accurate.

A practical testing sequence includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Export reports from the old system.
2. Complete a test migration.
3. Compare trial balances.
4. Compare balance sheets.
5. Compare profit and loss reports.
6. Check a sample of invoices, supplier bills and reconciled bank transactions.
7. Confirm accountant access and user permissions.
8. Only then begin day-to-day bookkeeping.

</div>

This verification process is repeatedly recommended in vendor guidance because mapping differences between software packages can produce subtle errors that are not immediately visible. Many small businesses can migrate successfully using built-in import tools, particularly when they have straightforward books and only a few years of history.

Professional assistance becomes much more valuable when a business has:
