## Introduction

[Buyer-intent keywords]({{ 'buyer-keywords/' | relative_url }}) are attractive because they reach people who appear close to making a purchase. Searches such as “best standing desk for home office”, “Product A vs Product B”, or “Brand X review” often convert far better than broad informational searches. The difficulty for affiliate websites is that everyone else recognises their value as well. Merchants, retailers, brands and competing affiliates frequently bid on the same searches, pushing advertising costs upwards through auction-based search advertising.

For affiliates, this creates a narrow economic window. Higher conversion rates do not automatically translate into higher profits if the cost of acquiring each visitor rises faster than the expected commission from each sale. Whether paid search is viable depends less on traffic volume than on the relationship between keyword cost, conversion rate and commission value. In many niches, expensive [buyer-intent]({{ 'buyer-intent/' | relative_url }}) keywords leave little room for profit unless [commissions]({{ 'commissions/' | relative_url }}) are unusually generous or conversion rates are exceptionally strong.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/buyer-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ahrefs.com">[Ahrefs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ahrefs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Buyer Intent Keywords Convert Better</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here&#x27;s How to Find...July 30, 2024 — 30 Jul 2024 — The most reliable way to find buyer-intent keywords is to use an SEO tool that allows...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why search markets price buying intent

Search advertising platforms operate as auctions. Advertisers bid because certain searches consistently produce revenue. Someone searching for "buy", "discount", "best", "review", "vs", or a specific product model has often progressed much further through the buying journey than someone searching for a general educational topic.

As more advertisers identify these commercial searches as profitable, competition increases. Google Ads does not simply reward the highest bidder, but intense competition generally raises the cost required to win impressions. High commercial intent therefore tends to correlate with higher cost-per-click (CPC), although actual prices vary by industry, competition and Quality Score.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ahrefs.com/blog/buyer-intent-keywords/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ahrefs.com">[Ahrefs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ahrefs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Buyer Intent Keywords Convert Better</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here&#x27;s How to Find...July 30, 2024 — 30 Jul 2024 — The most reliable way to find buyer-intent keywords is to use an SEO tool that allows...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2024</span></span></span>

For affiliates this creates an important distinction:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **High intent means higher potential revenue per visitor.**
* **High intent also usually means higher acquisition costs.**
* **The increase in conversion rate is not guaranteed to exceed the increase in advertising cost.**

</div>

This explains why experienced affiliates often evaluate expected [earnings per click]({{ 'earnings-per-click/' | relative_url }}) (EPC) before deciding whether to purchase traffic. A keyword that converts twice as well may still be unprofitable if each click costs three or four times more.

## Broad informational ads versus purchase-ready searches

The contrast between informational and transactional keywords illustrates why intent matters so much.

Informational searchBuyer-intent search"How does air frying work?""Best air fryer under £150""What is managed hosting?""SiteGround vs Bluehost""How to choose running shoes""Buy Brooks Ghost 17"

Informational searches typically attract users who are researching rather than buying immediately. They often cost less because fewer advertisers compete aggressively for them. However, they also produce lower short-term conversion rates because many users are still exploring options.

Purchase-ready searches generally attract users who have already narrowed their choices. These visitors are more likely to click affiliate links and complete purchases, but advertisers recognise this value and compete aggressively for the same traffic. ## Why affiliates often lose bidding wars

Merchants usually possess economic advantages that affiliates do not.

A retailer selling directly keeps the full gross margin from every sale and may also earn future repeat purchases from the same customer. Many businesses therefore calculate advertising returns using customer lifetime value rather than the profit from a single order.

An affiliate receives only a fraction of that value as commission.

For example:

* Retailer earns £80 gross profit on a sale.
* [Affiliate commission]({{ 'disclosure-967c81/' | relative_url }}) pays £8.
* Both compete for the same keyword.

The retailer can justify paying considerably more per click because each acquired customer may generate future purchases, email subscriptions or additional product sales. The affiliate normally receives only the single commission payment.

This structural difference explains why affiliates frequently find themselves priced out of the most competitive commercial keywords, particularly in sectors such as insurance, finance, legal services, software and consumer electronics, where advertisers are willing to pay very high CPCs. ## When high payouts can justify higher bids

High-intent keywords are not automatically poor choices for affiliates. They become viable when the economics support the advertising cost.

Higher bids become more defensible when several conditions align:

* **Large commissions.** Premium software subscriptions, business services or luxury products may generate commissions large enough to absorb expensive clicks.
* **Recurring revenue programmes.** Monthly revenue-share commissions increase the lifetime value of each referred customer.
* **Exceptional [merchant conversion]({{ 'merchant-cr/' | relative_url }}) rates.** If the merchant converts a high proportion of referred visitors, expected earnings per click increase.
* **Strong landing-page alignment.** A page that precisely answers the buyer's query can improve click-through rates to the merchant and reduce wasted traffic.

A keyword costing £6 per click may be impossible to justify for a product paying a £5 commission. The same keyword could be profitable for a software subscription paying £150 per successful referral.

The decision therefore depends on expected profit rather than CPC alone.

## The hidden risk of assuming intent guarantees profit

One common mistake is treating commercial intent as synonymous with profitability.

High intent improves the probability of conversion, but it does not eliminate uncertainty. Affiliates remain exposed to several variables outside their control:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* merchants may change commission rates;
* products may go out of stock;
* conversion rates fluctuate seasonally;
* commissions may be declined because of returns or cancelled subscriptions;
* competing advertisers can increase bids unexpectedly, raising CPC almost overnight.

</div>

Research on paid search consistently finds that campaign profitability depends on the interaction between CPC, conversion rate and acquisition cost rather than any single metric in isolation. ## Choosing intent selectively rather than chasing every buyer keyword

Experienced affiliates rarely attempt to buy every commercially valuable search term. Instead, they look for situations where purchase intent exists but competition is less intense.

Examples include:

* highly specific [long-tail]({{ 'long-tail/' | relative_url }}) product comparisons;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* niche products with fewer advertisers;
* budget-focused searches such as "under £200";
* specialised accessories rather than flagship products;
* emerging products before advertising competition becomes crowded.

</div>

These narrower searches often produce lower search volume but can offer a better balance between conversion potential and advertising cost.
