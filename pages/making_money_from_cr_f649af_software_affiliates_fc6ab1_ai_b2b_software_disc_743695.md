## Introduction

AI is changing how businesses discover software, but it is not eliminating the need for trusted affiliate review sites. Instead, it is changing what makes those sites valuable. Rather than reading ten comparison pages, many buyers now begin with AI-powered search tools or chatbots, then validate recommendations using review platforms, vendor documentation and independent expert opinions. For [software affiliates]({{ 'software/' | relative_url }}), this means success depends less on producing generic "Top 10" lists and more on publishing experience-based reviews that AI systems can confidently cite and buyers can trust. The opportunity remains significant, but the competitive advantage is moving from keyword coverage towards [credibility]({{ 'credibility/' | relative_url }}), first-hand testing and evidence-backed comparisons.

## How B2B buyers discover software now

The traditional software buying journey started with a Google search, followed by product websites and review platforms. Today the process is becoming more conversational.

A procurement manager might ask an AI assistant:

> "Which CRM works best for a manufacturing company with 50 employees using Microsoft 365?"

That query combines industry, company size, existing technology and business goals in a way that conventional keyword searches rarely did. AI systems then synthesise information from multiple sources before presenting a shortlist.

Research from G2 illustrates how quickly behaviour is changing. Its 2025 Buyer Behaviour Report found that software buyers increasingly rely on generative AI to produce tailored vendor shortlists, while later research reported that many buyers now begin software research with AI chatbots rather than traditional search engines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://images.g2crowd.com/uploads/attachment/file/1470753/2025-G2-Buyer-Behavior-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: images.g2crowd.com">[G2 Crowd Images+2G2]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">images.g2crowd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2025 G2 Buyer Behavior Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">G2 Crowd ImagesBuyer Behavior Report 20253 Jun 2025 — GenAI chatbots and software... This does not mean review platforms disappear. Instead, buyers typically combine several information sources:

* AI assistants for initial recommendations.
* Review platforms such as G2, Gartner Peer Insights, TrustRadius and Capterra for validation.
* Vendor documentation for features, pricing and [integrations]({{ 'integrations/' | relative_url }}).
* Independent reviewers who demonstrate real product experience.
* Sales conversations before making high-value purchasing decisions.

For expensive B2B software, buyers still need confidence before committing to contracts that may last years. AI often accelerates research rather than replacing it.

## What AI search rewards in review content

AI search systems differ from conventional search engines because they generate answers instead of simply ranking links. That changes which review pages become influential.

Google explains that its ranking systems continue to prioritise helpful, reliable, people-first content regardless of whether AI assisted with writing. The emphasis remains on demonstrating experience, expertise, [authority]({{ 'authority/' | relative_url }}) and trustworthiness rather than producing large volumes of content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: developers.google.com">[Google for Developers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">developers.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google for DevelopersCreating Helpful, Reliable, People-First ContentGoogle&#x27;s ranking systems are designed to present helpful, reliable i...</span></span></span>

For software [reviews]({{ 'reviews/' | relative_url }}), this generally means AI systems are more likely to rely on content that contains:

* First-hand product [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}).

* Original screenshots.
* Real implementation experiences.
* Specific strengths and weaknesses.
* Clear explanations of which businesses should and should not buy the software.
* Transparent discussion of pricing, limitations and alternatives.

Generic affiliate pages built from vendor marketing material contribute little unique information. They are less useful to both readers and AI systems looking for evidence.

Equally important is structure. AI systems extract concise facts such as pricing models, integrations, supported platforms, deployment options and feature comparisons more reliably when pages present information consistently instead of burying it inside promotional language.

## Why review platforms still matter

Many affiliate publishers assumed AI would replace software review sites. Current evidence suggests something more nuanced.

Review platforms continue to supply structured information that AI systems frequently reference. Industry analysis of AI Overview citations found platforms such as Gartner Peer Insights, G2 and Capterra among the most frequently cited sources because they aggregate verified reviews, feature comparisons and standardised product information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://seranking.com/blog/review-platforms-in-ai-overviews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: seranking.com">[SE Ranking]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">seranking.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">review platforms in ai overviews</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In our dataset, Gartner Peer Insights, G2, and Capterra remain in the top five...Read more...</span></span></span>

Their strengths include:

* Large numbers of verified customer reviews.
* Consistent feature taxonomies.
* Standard comparison formats.
* Vendor verification processes.
* Frequent [updates]({{ 'updates/' | relative_url }}).

However, review platforms also have limitations.

Reviews may reflect many different industries and use cases simultaneously. A five-star review from a multinational enterprise may have little relevance to a ten-person accounting firm. Independent affiliate sites can create value by interpreting products for specific audiences rather than simply aggregating ratings.

For example, a specialised article comparing project management software specifically for UK engineering consultancies offers context that a general review marketplace cannot easily provide.

## How affiliate pages can stay useful

The rise of AI changes optimisation priorities more than monetisation models.

Instead of attempting to rank for every software keyword, successful affiliate publishers increasingly benefit from becoming recognised experts within narrower software categories.

Practical approaches include:

**Test products personally.** Explain onboarding, migration, customer support and daily workflows rather than repeating marketing features.

**Publish comparison scenarios.** Instead of "HubSpot vs Salesforce", answer questions such as "Which CRM is better for a 20-person SaaS company with a limited sales team?"

**Include evidence.** Screenshots, benchmark data, pricing examples and implementation notes provide information unavailable from product brochures.

**Show trade-offs.** AI systems and human readers both benefit from balanced evaluations explaining where competitors outperform each other.

**Keep reviews current.** SaaS products evolve rapidly through monthly releases, making outdated affiliate pages progressively less valuable.

**Explain who should avoid the product.** Honest exclusions often increase trust more than universal recommendations.

This type of content is considerably harder for competitors to automate at scale.

## The growing importance of quotable expertise

AI systems increasingly synthesise rather than reproduce entire articles. That makes concise, evidence-based observations especially valuable.

For example, statements such as:

> "Teams under twenty people usually gain more value from Product A's simpler onboarding than Product B's advanced automation."

provide clear judgement that can support AI-generated answers because they express contextual expertise instead of generic marketing language.

Similarly, documenting implementation experiences—how long setup required, unexpected migration issues or customer support quality—creates information unavailable from vendor websites.

Experience has become a competitive asset rather than merely a credibility signal.

## Traffic may decline while influence grows

One of the largest changes facing affiliate publishers is that visibility and [traffic]({{ 'traffic/' | relative_url }}) are becoming less closely linked.

AI-generated answers often satisfy users without requiring a click. Industry observers have described this trend as "Google Zero", reflecting concerns that AI-generated summaries reduce visits to publishers despite using their information. Studies also suggest many searches already end without clicking through to external websites, with AI Overviews accelerating that pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/what-is-google-zero-and-why-your-favorite-websites-are-panicking-about-ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tomsguide.com">[Tom&#x27;s Guide]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tomsguide.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tom&#x27;s Guide What is Google Zero</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">These features summarize answers directly on the search page, drastically reducing the need for users to click through to original conten...</span></span></span>

For affiliate businesses, fewer visitors do not necessarily mean less commercial impact.

A review that becomes a trusted source for AI summaries may influence purchasing decisions earlier in the buying process, even if fewer users visit the page directly. High-intent traffic may become more valuable than large volumes of casual visitors.

That shifts performance measurement away from page views alone towards conversions, assisted purchases and brand recognition.

## What this means for software affiliate businesses

AI is unlikely to eliminate software affiliate sites because enterprise software purchases remain high-risk decisions involving budgets, implementation effort and organisational change.

Instead, AI is raising the standard.

The affiliate pages most likely to succeed will resemble independent analysts rather than content farms. They will publish original testing, explain real business trade-offs, update reviews regularly and provide context that neither vendors nor large review platforms can fully replicate.

As buyers increasingly combine AI assistants with review marketplaces and human expertise, affiliate publishers who produce genuinely experience-based content are more likely to remain visible, earn citations and convert readers into recurring software customers.
